## Why Data Science Matters in Social and Interdisciplinary Contexts

To me, learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts is important because data is connected to almost everything we do. People create data every day through the choices they make, the things they buy, the places they go, the sports they play, and even the things they search for online. I think it is important to understand that the numbers we work with often represent real people and real situations. Data science gives us a way to look at those numbers, find patterns, and use them to better understand the world around us.

I think modeling social topics and people matters because it can help us understand things that we might not notice on our own. Data can be used by businesses to understand their customers, sports teams to understand player performance, and financial organizations to make better decisions. It can also help us understand larger topics like education, income, employment, and access to resources. With my background in Computer Science and my minor in Economics, I like that data science allows me to bring different subjects together. I can use programming, statistics, and economics to look at problems from different perspectives.

I also think there are a lot of responsibilities that come with using data about people. Just because we have access to someone's information does not mean we should use it however we want. Privacy is important, and I think data scientists need to be careful about how they collect, store, and use information. Another responsibility is understanding bias. If the data we are using is biased, the results we get from that data can also be biased. I think it is important to question the data and understand where it comes from instead of assuming that numbers always tell the entire story.

Through this course, I am hoping to better understand how data can be used to explain real-world situations and help people make better decisions. I want to become better at finding patterns, understanding what those patterns actually mean, and communicating my findings to other people. I am especially interested in applying what I learn to areas like finance, business, sports, and potentially animals or marine life. Overall, I want to learn how to look beyond the numbers and understand the story behind them while continuing to build skills that I can use in my future career.
